The Stories So Far

Shelton Outlasts Tiafoe in an All-American Classic to Reach His First Major Final

SHELTON [8] def. TIAFOE [11] — 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 (Men's SF2)

Frances Tiafoe took the opening set, but Ben Shelton settled in and took the next two 6-3, 6-3 to turn the match around. Tiafoe pushed back in the fourth, but Shelton closed it out 7-5 to reach his first career Grand Slam final. It guarantees an American finalist regardless of what happens Sunday's a first for the men's game since Andy Roddick won the title here in 2003.

Shelton now meets top seed Alexander Zverev in Sunday's final at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

Zverev Survives Two Tiebreaks to Reach a Third Straight Grand Slam Final

ZVEREV [1] def. KHACHANOV — 6-3, 7-6(7), 7-6(6) (Men's SF1)

Alexander Zverev won in straight sets, but the scoreline flatters him. He took the opening set 6-3 with ease, but unseeded Karen Khachanov pushed him to the limit from there, forcing back-to-back tiebreaks. Khachanov even erased two match points in the second-set breaker before Zverev finally closed it out, and led the deciding tiebreak 4-1 with two set points of his own before Zverev reeled off the last points to seal it.

It is Zverev's third consecutive Grand Slam final of 2026, following his Roland Garros title and his Wimbledon final defeat. He will play Ben Shelton on Sunday, looking to avenge his 2020 US Open final loss.

Sabalenka Breaks Through Pegula in a High-Power Semifinal

SABALENKA [1] def. PEGULA [3] — 7-5, 6-2 (Women's SF1)

Aryna Sabalenka is back in the US Open final for a fourth consecutive year. Jessica Pegula stayed with the defending champion through most of the opening set, using accurate first serves and quick, flat exchanges to keep the match level. Sabalenka finally turned the pressure into a breakthrough at 5-5, winning the last two games to take the first set 7-5.

The second set was a different story. Sabalenka increased the pressure on Pegula's second serve and repeatedly attacked from the baseline. She broke in the fourth game to move ahead 3-1, then added another break at 5-2 before closing out the match in 80 minutes. She finished with seven aces and 29 winners against 20 unforced errors, while Pegula could not find another break-point chance after her lone opportunity late in the first set.

The victory sends Sabalenka into a fourth consecutive US Open final and leaves her one win away from a three-peat. She will face Elena Rybakina in Saturday's final.

Rybakina Comes Back to Beat Gauff and Reach the US Open Final

RYBAKINA [2] def. GAUFF [4] — 3-6, 6-4, 6-4 (Women's SF2)

Coco Gauff made the stronger start and took the opening set 6-3, using aggressive returns and a much-improved serve to put Elena Rybakina under pressure. The new world No. 1 responded in the second set, finding more success against Gauff's forehand and leveling the match 6-4, as Gauff's error count began to climb.

The deciding set stayed close, but Gauff's serve and forehand continued to break down under the pressure. Rybakina steadied herself, capitalized on the mistakes, and closed out the third set 6-4 to complete the comeback. Rybakina now advances to her first US Open final, where she will face defending champion Aryna Sabalenka on Saturday.

Shelton Stuns Alcaraz in a Match That Ended at 3:33 a.m. — the Latest in US Open History

SHELTON [8] def. ALCARAZ [2] (Men's QF)

Ben Shelton had never beaten Carlos Alcaraz in three previous meetings. Wednesday night at Arthur Ashe he beat him in five sets, in a match that finished at 3:33 a.m. ET — the latest finish in US Open history. Alcaraz won the first set in a tiebreak, Shelton owned the second and third, and Alcaraz took the fourth convincingly before Shelton closed it out in a deciding-set tiebreak. When the match point landed, Shelton let out the roar Flushing Meadows had been waiting for.

This ended Alcaraz's 18-match Grand Slam winning streak. Shelton would go on to beat Tiafoe in the all-American men's semifinal to reach his first major final.

Tiafoe Comes Back from 0-2 in Sets to Beat Michelsen in Five

TIAFOE [11] def. MICHELSEN (Men's QF)

Two sets down against Alex Michelsen, Frances Tiafoe looked like he was heading out. Instead he won three consecutive sets, taking the decisive fifth-set tiebreak. He went on to meet Ben Shelton in the most anticipated match of the tournament: two Americans, one guaranteed finalist, and Arthur Ashe roaring behind both of them.

Historic: Four Americans in Grand Slam Semis — All Top-4 Women Seeds Through

HISTORIC — Gauff [4], Pegula [3], Tiafoe [11], Shelton [8] all in the last four

Four Americans in four Grand Slam semifinals. The men's draw guaranteed a first American Grand Slam finalist since Roddick in 2003. On the women's side: Gauff and Pegula, and all four top seeds made the semis — the first time that has happened at the US Open since 1975. Either an American or the newly-crowned World No. 1 will win the women's title.

Zverev Yet to Drop a Set — Is He Unbeatable?

ZVEREV [1] def. VAN DE ZANDSCHULP (Men's QF)

Alexander Zverev had not dropped a single set all tournament. He dispatched van de Zandschulp without being tested ahead of his semifinal against Karen Khachanov. Zverev had been in two Grand Slam finals and lost both. He had never looked more ready to close one out — and three days later, he did.

Sinner Out Before It Started — Djokovic Falls in Round 1

EARLY EXITS — No. 1 Sinner (knee), Djokovic R1 loss to Navone

World No. 1 Jannik Sinner withdrew before the tournament with a right knee injury. Novak Djokovic was eliminated in the first round by Mariano Navone — only his third career first-round loss in a major, and the first time none of the Big Three reached Round 2 since the 2003 French Open. Both exits opened the draw wide, and the Americans walked through it.

Storylines Heading Into the Finals

FINAL · MEN'S SF2

Shelton Beats Tiafoe: First US Men's Major Finalist Since Roddick

Frances Tiafoe took the opening set, but Ben Shelton won three of the next four to reach his first Grand Slam final, 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5. It guarantees an American finalist — the first for the men's game since Andy Roddick won right here in 2003. Shelton now plays top seed Alexander Zverev on Sunday.

FINAL · WOMEN'S SF2

Rybakina Comes Back to Beat Gauff in Three Sets

Coco Gauff took the opening set 6-3, but Elena Rybakina responded by winning the second and third sets 6-4, 6-4. The new World No. 1 held her nerve in the closing stages to reach the US Open final, where she now faces defending champion Aryna Sabalenka, live at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

FINAL · WOMEN'S SF1

Sabalenka Defeats Pegula 7-5, 6-2 to Reach Fourth Straight US Open Final

Aryna Sabalenka converted her pre-match edge into a straight-sets victory over Jessica Pegula. She won 7-5, 6-2 in 80 minutes, breaking Pegula three times and advancing to her fourth consecutive US Open final.

FINAL · MEN'S SF1

Zverev Escapes Two Tiebreaks to Beat Khachanov

Alexander Zverev hadn't dropped a set all tournament and still didn't — but Karen Khachanov made him work for every point, taking both the second and third sets to tiebreaks. Zverev prevailed 6-3, 7-6(7), 7-6(6) to reach his third straight Grand Slam final, and will play Ben Shelton on Sunday.
