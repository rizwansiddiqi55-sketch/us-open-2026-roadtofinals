Latest Match Story

Zverev Holds His Nerve to Reach the US Open Final

ZVEREV [1] def. KHACHANOV — 6-3, 7-6, 7-6 (Men's SF1)

Alexander Zverev is through to the US Open final after defeating Karen Khachanov in straight sets at Arthur Ashe Stadium. The top seed took control early with a 6-3 opening set before edging a tense second-set tiebreak 7-6.

The third set was another test of composure, with neither player able to create separation through the regular games. Zverev again prevailed in the tiebreak to close out a hard-fought victory and book his place in the championship match.

Zverev now awaits the winner of the all-American semifinal between Frances Tiafoe and Ben Shelton. His victory also continues his remarkable 2026 Grand Slam run after winning Roland Garros and reaching the Wimbledon final.

The Stories So Far

Sabalenka Breaks Through Pegula in a High-Power Semifinal

SABALENKA [1] def. PEGULA [3] — 7-5, 6-2 (Women's SF1)

Aryna Sabalenka is back in the US Open final for a fourth consecutive year. Jessica Pegula stayed with the defending champion through most of the opening set, using accurate first serves and quick, flat exchanges to keep the match level. Sabalenka finally turned the pressure into a breakthrough at 5-5, winning the last two games to take the first set 7-5.

The second set was a different story. Sabalenka increased the pressure on Pegula's second serve and repeatedly attacked from the baseline. She broke in the fourth game to move ahead 3-1, then added another break at 5-2 before closing out the match in 80 minutes. She finished with seven aces and 29 winners against 20 unforced errors, while Pegula could not find another break-point chance after her lone opportunity late in the first set.

The victory sends Sabalenka into a fourth consecutive US Open final and leaves her one win away from a three-peat. She will face Elena Rybakina in Saturday's final.

Rybakina Comes Back to Beat Gauff and Reach the US Open Final

RYBAKINA [2] def. GAUFF [4] — 3-6, 6-4, 6-4 (Women's SF2)

Coco Gauff made the stronger start and took the opening set 6-3, using aggressive returns and a much-improved serve to put Elena Rybakina under pressure. The new world No. 1 responded in the second set, finding more success against Gauff's forehand and leveling the match 6-4, as Gauff's error count began to climb.

The deciding set stayed close, but Gauff's serve and forehand continued to break down under the pressure. Rybakina steadied herself, capitalized on the mistakes, and closed out the third set 6-4 to complete the comeback. Rybakina now advances to her first US Open final, where she will face defending champion Aryna Sabalenka on Saturday.

Shelton Stuns Alcaraz in a Match That Ended at 3:33 a.m. — the Latest in US Open History

SHELTON [8] def. ALCARAZ [2] — 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(7) (Men's QF)

Ben Shelton had never beaten Carlos Alcaraz in three previous meetings. Wednesday night at Arthur Ashe he beat him in five sets, in a match that finished at 3:33 a.m. ET — the latest finish in US Open history. Alcaraz won the first set in a tiebreak, Shelton owned the second and third, Alcaraz took the fourth convincingly. The fifth set went to 7-6(7). When the match point landed, Shelton let out the roar Flushing Meadows had been waiting for.

This ended Alcaraz's 18-match Grand Slam winning streak. Shelton now plays Tiafoe in the all-American men's semifinal on Friday. An American man is guaranteed in the final — the first since Andy Roddick won here in 2003.

Tiafoe Comes Back from 0-2 in Sets to Beat Michelsen in Five

TIAFOE [11] def. MICHELSEN — 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6, 7-6(10-6) (Men's QF)

Two sets down against Alex Michelsen, Frances Tiafoe looked like he was heading out. Instead he won three consecutive sets, taking the decisive fifth-set tiebreak 7-6(6). He meets Ben Shelton in what is the most anticipated match of the tournament: two Americans, one guaranteed finalist, and Arthur Ashe roaring behind both of them.

Historic: Four Americans in Grand Slam Semis — All Top-4 Women Seeds Through

HISTORIC — Gauff [4], Pegula [3], Tiafoe [11], Shelton [8] all in the last four

Four Americans in four Grand Slam semifinals. The men's draw guarantees a first American Grand Slam finalist since Roddick in 2003. On the women's side: Gauff and Pegula, and all four top seeds made the semis — the first time that has happened at the US Open since 1975. Either an American or the newly-crowned World No. 1 will win the women's title.

Zverev Yet to Drop a Set — Is He Unbeatable?

ZVEREV [1] def. VAN DE ZANDSCHULP — 6-2, 7-5, 6-1 (Men's QF)

Alexander Zverev has not dropped a single set all tournament. He dispatched van de Zandschulp without being tested and faces Karen Khachanov in the men's semifinal Friday. Zverev has been in two Grand Slam finals and lost both. He has never looked more ready to close one out.

Sinner Out Before It Started — Djokovic Falls in Round 1

EARLY EXITS — No. 1 Sinner (knee), Djokovic R1 loss to Navone

World No. 1 Jannik Sinner withdrew before the tournament with a right knee injury. Novak Djokovic was eliminated in the first round by Mariano Navone — only his third career first-round loss in a major, and the first time none of the Big Three reached Round 2 since the 2003 French Open. Both exits opened the draw wide, and the Americans walked through it.

Four Storylines Heading Into Semis

ALL-AMERICAN CLASH

Tiafoe vs. Shelton: One of Them Makes the First US Men's Major Final Since Roddick

Frances Tiafoe has reached three US Open semifinals. Ben Shelton just beat the defending champion at 3:33 a.m. One of them will play Sunday's men's final — the first American man in a Grand Slam final since Andy Roddick won right here in 2003. The crowd will be split. The atmosphere will be extraordinary.

FINAL · WOMEN'S SF2

Rybakina Comes Back to Beat Gauff in Three Sets

Coco Gauff took the opening set 6-3, but Elena Rybakina responded by winning the second and third sets 6-4, 6-4. The new World No. 1 held her nerve in the closing stages to reach the US Open final, where she will face defending champion Aryna Sabalenka.

Aryna Sabalenka converted her pre-match edge into a straight-sets victory over Jessica Pegula. She won 7-5, 6-2 in 80 minutes, breaking Pegula three times and advancing to her fourth consecutive US Open final.

Alexander Zverev has not dropped a set all tournament. He faces Karen Khachanov, who has had an unexpected run. Zverev is the heavy favourite but has twice been to Grand Slam finals and walked away without the trophy. Friday is the next test.
