Fitness Tips & Warm-Up

Tennis asks for sudden sprints, stops, twists and overhead hits. A few minutes of preparation before you play and some easy stretching afterwards lowers the chance of strains and overuse injuries. This is general fitness information, not medical advice.

💪 Fitness tips for tennis players

Tennis mixes short sprints, quick changes of direction and long points, so fitness for it is a blend of legs, core, shoulders and stamina. These are general tips for recreational players, not a personal training plan.

🏃 Stamina

Build a base with 2–3 easy cardio sessions a week (jog, bike or swim, 20–40 min). Add intervals once a week: 20 seconds hard, 40 seconds easy, 8–10 rounds, to match the stop-start pattern of points.

🦵 Leg strength

Squats, lunges, step-ups and calf raises twice a week. Strong legs power the serve and protect the knees and ankles. Start with body weight, then add light weights.

🧘 Core and balance

Planks, side planks, Russian twists and single-leg balance work. A strong core links the legs to the swing and helps protect the lower back.

💪 Shoulders and arms

Band external rotations, rows and push-ups keep the shoulder stable for serving. Add forearm work (wrist curls and extensions) to look after the elbow.

⚡ Speed and footwork

Short ladder drills, side shuffles, split steps and cone sprints, 10 minutes after a warm-up. Quality matters more than volume, so stop when your footwork gets sloppy.

💧 Hydration and food

Drink water through the day and sip during play, adding electrolytes for sessions over an hour or in the heat. Eat carbohydrate and a little protein 2–3 hours before, and a snack with protein within an hour after.

😴 Sleep and recovery

Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep. Keep one or two easy days a week, and take a lighter day after a hard match. Soreness that fades in a day or two is normal; sharp or lingering pain is not.

👟 Shoes and kit

Wear proper court shoes for your surface, and replace them when the tread is smooth or the cushioning feels flat. Match your grip size and string tension to your arm comfort.

Check with a doctor first if you have a medical condition, are returning after an injury, or have not exercised for a long time. Build up slowly: raise your total weekly load by no more than about 10% at a time.

⏰ Before you play — 8 to 10 minutes, dynamic

Move through the motions you will use. Save long held stretches for afterwards, since holding them for a long time right before play can leave muscles feeling flat.

  1. Easy jog or skipping2 min

    Light jog or skipping on the spot to raise your heart rate gently.

  2. Leg swings10 each leg

    Hold a fence or wall. Swing one leg front to back, then side to side, keeping the hips level.

  3. Walking lunges with a twist8 each side

    Step into a lunge and rotate your upper body over the front leg. Keep the front knee over the ankle.

  4. Arm circles10 + 10

    Small circles growing to big ones, forward then backward, with relaxed shoulders.

  5. Shoulder opener12 reps

    Pull a light band apart, or squeeze your shoulder blades back with elbows bent.

  6. Trunk rotations10 each side

    Feet planted, turn your hips and shoulders together, arms relaxed across the chest.

  7. Wrist and forearm circles10 each way

    Circle both wrists, then open and close your hands to warm the forearms.

  8. Side shuffles and cross-steps2 baseline lengths

    Stay low, push off the outside foot and move sideways without crossing the feet, then add cross-steps.

  9. Mini-tennis3-5 min

    Easy rallies from the service line, then the baseline, building the pace slowly.

  10. Serve build-up10-15 serves

    Shadow serves first, then half pace, then full pace once the shoulder feels loose.

⚡ The “microwave” warm-up (idea from Brad Gilbert’s Winning Ugly)

In his book Winning Ugly, coach Brad Gilbert argues that your match starts before you reach the court, and that the short on-court knock-up can do two jobs: get your strokes ready fast, and tell you about your opponent. This is a short summary in our own words, so read the book for his full explanation.

🧠 1. Start in your head

Warming up begins with your mindset on the way to the match: decide your game plan, and keep your focus on what you can control.

🎾 2. Quick strokes, in order

  • Groundstrokes on both sides, hit deep and with purpose
  • A few volleys
  • Overheads, which also help get the serve ready
  • Serves, then returns

👁 3. Scout while you warm up

Watch how your opponent moves and which wing, grip and serve look weaker. The knock-up is a free chance to gather information.

⚠️ 4. Body first

A quick on-court routine is not a replacement for the dynamic warm-up above. Do the 8–10 minute movement warm-up first, especially in cold weather or if you are stiff, then use the quick routine to find your timing.

🛁 After you play — 5 to 10 minutes, static

Walk for 2–3 minutes first. Hold each stretch for 20–30 seconds, twice. It should feel like a gentle pull, never sharp pain.

  1. Cross-body shoulder20-30 s x 2

    Pull one arm across your chest with the other hand, keeping the shoulder down.

  2. Overhead triceps20-30 s x 2

    Hand behind your head, use the other hand to press the elbow gently back.

  3. Doorway chest stretch20-30 s x 2

    Forearm on a door frame, step through slightly until you feel the chest open.

  4. Wrist flexors and extensors20-30 s each

    Arm straight in front, palm up then palm down, and gently bend the hand with the other hand.

  5. Standing quad20-30 s each

    Pull your heel towards your glute, knees together, standing tall. Hold a wall if you need.

  6. Hamstring20-30 s each

    Heel on a low step, knee soft, hinge forward from the hips with a flat back.

  7. Calf20-30 s each

    Hands on a wall, back leg straight with the heel pressed down, front knee bent.

  8. Hip flexor lunge20-30 s each

    Kneeling or low lunge, tuck your pelvis under and lean forward slightly.

  9. Glute stretch (figure-4)20-30 s each

    Standing at a wall or seated, cross one ankle over the opposite knee and sit back.

  10. Lower back and trunk side bend20-30 s each side

    Reach one arm overhead and lean to the opposite side, keeping both hips facing forward.

Common tennis injuries and how to lower the risk

💪 Tennis elbow

Pain on the outside of the elbow. Strengthen the forearm with slow wrist extensions, check your grip size and string tension (softer strings are kinder), and avoid long sessions with heavy, wet balls.

🎾 Shoulder (serve)

Overhead hitting loads the rotator cuff. Do band external rotations and shoulder-blade exercises twice a week, and build up the number of serves gradually instead of jumping from none to a hundred.

🦶 Ankle sprains

Wear court shoes that still have grip and cushioning, practise single-leg balance, and replace worn shoes. Avoid playing on a wet or dusty court.

🦵 Calf, knee and Achilles

Calf raises and lunges build strength; add playing time gradually and keep a rest day between hard sessions. Tight calves and sudden speed work are a common mix for strains.

🦯 Lower back

Train the core and hip mobility, and keep the serve rhythmic instead of over-arching the back. Stretch the hip flexors after play.

🌞 Heat and dehydration

In the UAE, play early or late in the day, drink water before and during play (add electrolytes for long sessions), wear a cap and sun protection, and stop if you feel dizzy.

Stop and get checked if you have sharp or worsening pain, swelling, numbness, a joint that gives way, or pain that lingers for days. See a doctor or physiotherapist. Anyone with a medical condition should check with a doctor before starting a new routine.
